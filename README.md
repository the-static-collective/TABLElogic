## Why this repository exists

The Static Collective already has computational organs. The missing interface is the **encounter**: a place where independently attributable particulars can be made mutually legible without being prematurely merged into a single truth, intention, plan, or authority.

A table is both:

- a data structure for addressing particulars and typed relations; and
- an inhabitable meeting place where people, computational organs, and resources can encounter one another.

**TABLElogic is a candidate grammar for encounters and crossings.** It should define the smallest common questions and receipts, then return execution and decisions to the owning parties.

It is **not** another orchestrator, a sovereign brain, or a replacement for the mathematics already developed in nuMathelology / nunuMATH.

The table may suggest a relation. **It may not declare consent, evidence, or actual performance on the participants' behalf.** The absence of a connection is also meaningful: a gap is not permission to invent a bridge.

## Candidate laws (not yet proven shared laws)

1. **PARTICULAR ≠ PROJECTION.** A view of a participant is not the participant.
2. **ADDRESSABLE ≠ ADMITTED.** A thing may be named or considered without becoming a fact.
3. **POSSIBILITY ≠ PROPOSAL ≠ INVITATION ≠ CONSENT ≠ EXECUTION.**
4. **RELATION ≠ OWNERSHIP.** A successful encounter does not transfer sovereignty.
5. **SAME ENDPOINT ≠ SAME FORMATION.** The operator and route travel with the receipt.
6. **AMBIGUITY IS INFORMATION.** Hold multiple possible readings until evidence or a participant resolves what matters.
7. **REFUSAL IS AN EVENT.** A refusal may leave attributable history without performing the proposed action.
8. **THE TABLE MAY GROW; ITS PARTICIPANTS MAY LEAVE.**

These are proposed interface rules, not claims that every donor repository currently implements them.

## First falsifiable encounter: a community table

Use fictional, independently attributable records:

- One gardener reports an available surplus and an offer to share it.
- One organizer reports a need for food at a gathering.
- An attendant proposes a possible match.
- Dogram checks a declared compatibility predicate, not the truth of either claim.
- One participant declines. The refusal must survive without any transfer.
- In a second replay, both parties explicitly consent, an owner-authorized mock action occurs, and a reLATTE-shaped receipt identifies the exact occurrence.
- A later query must distinguish *suggested*, *accepted*, *performed*, and *witnessed*.
- An ambiguous utterance and an unverified resource must remain unresolved rather than silently converted to permission or inventory.

**Pass condition:** a cold re-reader can reconstruct who proposed what, what was admitted, what actually occurred, what remains unknown, and which particular source/organ owned every step.

**Failure condition:** any projection, model completion, score, or registry entry gets treated as an authorization or a real-world accomplishment.

## Architecture direction

Use existing repo-owned contracts and exact source pins. Prefer a tiny typed fixture and independent verifier over a new service framework. Start read-only and simulated, then cross into owner-controlled runtimes only through separate permissioned integrations.

This repository is the **tablecloth**, not the feast.
